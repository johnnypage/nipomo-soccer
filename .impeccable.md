## Design Context

### Users
Parents in Nipomo, CA (small town, ~18k pop) evaluating recreational soccer for their kids ages 2 through high school. They're on phones at the kitchen table, comparing this to AYSO, deciding whether to commit $150+ and multiple evenings a week. Many are returning families who already know the coaches. The 5v5 page also targets busy teens' parents who need to see "no practices" immediately.

### Brand Personality
Community-proud, athletic, direct. Not corporate, not overly casual. The energy of a well-run local sports organization that takes the sport seriously but keeps it fun. Think: your neighbor who happens to run a really sharp program.

### Aesthetic Direction
- **Tone**: Athletic energy. Parents should feel "my kid is going to love this." Dynamic, sport-forward, action-first.
- **Theme**: Dark-dominant with light section alternation. 80/20 dark/light per brand rules. Night backgrounds with warmwhite/paper breaks.
- **Colors**: Crimson (#8B2332) primary, Gold (#D4A747) accent, Night (#0D0D0D) background, Warmwhite (#F4EDE1) text, Paper (#F2F2EE) light sections. No gradients (brand rule).
- **Typography**: Integral CF (athletic block sans, always uppercase) for display/headlines. Montserrat for subheadings. Inter for body. These are established and non-negotiable.
- **Anti-references**: Generic youth sports sites with stock photos and Comic Sans. Corporate SaaS landing pages. Anything that looks like a template.
- **References**: Nike community sports pages. LAFC community section. Well-designed local club sites that feel like they have a real identity.

### Design Principles
1. **Sport-forward**: The design should feel athletic, not administrative. Every section should evoke game day energy.
2. **Scannable confidence**: Parents are skimming. Key facts (price, schedule, format) must be instantly findable without reading paragraphs.
3. **Local identity**: This is Nipomo's thing. The design should feel specific and owned, not templated.
4. **Earned hierarchy**: Not every section is equal. Hero and division selection are the main events. FAQ and pricing are supporting.
5. **Mobile-native**: Most parents hit this on their phone. Design for the thumb, not the mouse.
